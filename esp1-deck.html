After-Stage Debrief: Keep the Lesson. Drop the Emotion.

  1. What did I see well?
    Identify one successful visual behavior, not only the score.
  2. Where did vision disappear?
    Find the moment attention jumped to speed, outcome, or panic.
  3. What is one controllable adjustment?
    Keep it small enough to use on the next stage.
  4. What is my next cue?
    Choose one cue only. Then close the stage.

45-Second Make Ready Bubble Protocol

Use this after your final stage plan is decided. Once you start this, stop redesigning the stage.

  1. Load deliberately.
    Perform the IPSC command sequence safely and cleanly. This is your entry point into the bubble.
  2. Exhale once.
    Let the shoulders drop. Keep the grip intention high, but remove unnecessary face, neck, and jaw tension.
  3. See the first sight picture.
    Not the whole stage. Not the score. Just the first confirmed visual event.
  4. Commit to one cue.
    Use one sentence only: Eyes lead. Sights confirm. Trigger follows.
  5. Accept the noise.
    Nerves, RO, timer, squad, match pressure: all of it can exist while your eyes do the job.
  6. Enter on purpose.
    At the beep, do not chase speed. Let the eyes arrive first and let the gun follow.

IPSC Core Rules for This Deck

Speed is earned by seeing sooner, not by confirming less.

Draw: Establish grip before extension. The draw ends in a usable sight picture, not just an extended gun.

Double tap: Treat it as two sight pictures and two shots. The second shot is governed by sight return.

Transitions: The eyes leave first. The gun follows. The shot breaks only when the confirmation is sufficient.

Entries and exits: Arrive ready to see. Leave only after the required visual work is complete.

Red flag: If you remember the plan but not the sights, you were running the stage instead of shooting it.

Training modes

Mode 1: Dry Fire — 10 minutes

Draw five cards. For each card, perform 6–10 perfect dry repetitions. End every repetition by naming exactly what you saw: front sight, dot, A-zone, hardcover edge, steel plate, or exit point.

Mode 2: Live Fire — Low round count

Pick one card only. Shoot 3–5 runs of a simple drill. Evaluate only the cue. Do not mix technical correction, score analysis, and mental training in the same repetition.

Mode 3: Match Day — Before first stage

Use the Make Ready Bubble Protocol. Pick one cue for the first stage. After the stage, write one sentence: “I saw…” or “I rushed…” Then reset.

Mode 4: Mistake Recovery

If you make a mistake, do not audit your personality. Name the next visual task and continue. The next target deserves full attention.

Research translation

This deck translates research from quiet-eye training, gaze control, attentional focus, pre-performance routines, choking under pressure, and sport psychology into IPSC-specific cue cards. It also uses IPSC terminology and teaching points from IPSC godkjenningskurs by Espen Fiskebeck, including sight picture, sight return, two-hand grip, draw, reload, target transitions, movement, entries/exits, and the Load and Make Ready start sequence.

The practical message is simple: under pressure, do not add thoughts. Reduce the task to a stable routine and one visual job at a time.
